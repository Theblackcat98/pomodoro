Center circle  
A ring slowly closes like an analog timer. Inside, large digits count down. Under them, one word: “Focus” or “Break”.  
Below the ring, one button that simply says START. When running it says PAUSE. Nothing else vies for your attention.

Quick choices  
A quiet row of gentle capsules sits under the button: 15, 25, 35, custom. Tapping a capsule instantly changes the ring’s duration; no confirm, no save.

Tasks, if you want them  
A slim bar under the capsules says “Add a task”. Type, hit enter, the bar collapses into a tidy checklist. Each task is a pale card with a hollow circle. Tick the circle when the task is done; swipe to delete. No urgency, no gamified confetti—just a place to park your intention.

Sound and silence  
A tiny headphone icon in the corner toggles café ambience. When the ring closes, a soft bell or a phone vibration announces the end, then automatically offers a short break. If you denied notifications, a polite banner appears once: “We’ll nudge you at the end—allow?” Dismiss and it never nags again.

Stopwatch mode  
Open the drawer and choose Stopwatch. The same ring becomes a plain upward counter, a lap button appears, and previous laps stack beneath in a scrollable list. It feels like the timer, but time flows upward instead of down.

Lists view  
The drawer also holds Lists. Here tasks live without the timer. You can reorder by drag, add subtasks with the return key, or mark a task “done” with a satisfying check animation. Each task may carry tiny tomato icons showing how many pomodoros you estimated versus completed—visual feedback on planning accuracy.

Statistics  
Tap the tomato counter on the main card and you slide into a calm dashboard: today’s pomodoros, total focus time, a small calendar heat-map of past days. No leaderboards, no social badges—just enough data to notice patterns.

Settings  
A gear in the drawer opens a quiet sheet: choose dark or light, pick bell or mute, decide whether breaks auto-start, export your data as plain text. Every change applies instantly; there is no “save”.

Help  
A question-mark row in the drawer reveals a searchable set of plain-language answers: What is a pomodoro? How do I use the keyboard? Why are my notifications silent? The first time you open the app a short, dismissible carousel explains the basics and asks for notification permission once.

Offline life  
The app keeps working if the network dies. Your tasks, timer state, and completed pomodoros sit in the browser; when the connection returns they sync quietly in the background.

The whole experience is intentionally monastic: one focal circle, one primary button, and an optional list. Color is limited to the tomato red of the ring and gentle greens or ambers for task borders. There are no footers, no upsells, no account walls. The app looks like a mindful breath—simple, visible, and out of the way—so you can close the tab when the work is done.
